Wavelength coverage and resolution

Summarize the wavelength coverage and resolution by each grating.

Every NGSL target was observed identically: three STIS CCD low-dispersion gratings through the 52X0.2E1 slit, spliced into one continuous spectrum per star. The delivered product hides the seams — there is a single wavelength array — but the native sampling of each grating survives in it, so the segments can be recovered by differencing the wavelength column.

The three dispersions below are measured from the delivered grid, not quoted from the instrument handbook. The splice points fall inside the overlap regions the pipeline averaged across: 2990–3060 Å for G230LB/G430L and 5500–5650 Å for G430L/G750L.

Sampling is not resolution, though, and for these modes the two pull in opposite directions. The second table below gives the actual resolution, measured from the STIS model line spread functions at NGSL’s 52x0.2 aperture.

Values at the segment ends and the Balmer limit are interpolated between the two tabulated G430L points, whose FWHM varies slowly and near-linearly. Rows marked * are the NUV-MAMA G230L, the only 230-nm LSF that page provides; FWHM is given in pixels only, since converting it would require the MAMA dispersion from a different detector.

The spectra are undersampled, so the real resolution beats the pixel grid. The STIS CCD LSF is only 1.39–1.66 pixels FWHM through the 0.2″ slit — narrower than the two-pixel Nyquist element. Actual resolving power therefore runs 20–44% higher than the sampling alone implies: 804 → 1343 across G430L against 557 → 1029 from sampling. At the Balmer break, R ≈ 939, not the 664 the pixel grid suggests.
What this means for fitting the break. Convolve models with the tabulated LSF, not a Gaussian of 2 pixels — the latter over-broadens by ~40% at 3646 Å and will make a correct model look too sharp. R still varies strongly with wavelength (it climbs across each grating and resets at every splice), so a single R across 1675–10198 Å is wrong everywhere; use a wavelength-dependent kernel. And because the LSF is narrower than 2 pixels, the data are formally undersampled: sharp features are aliased, and interpolating onto a finer grid recovers nothing real. Note also that G230LB has no published LSF on that page, so the UV segment’s true resolution remains the one unquantified piece — the sampling-limited 610 → 1114 is a lower bound there.

Signal to noise

Summarize the typical signal to noise.

This is the one place where the delivered files will mislead you if taken at face value. The STATERR column contains propagated counting statistics only. Measured against the actual pixel-to-pixel scatter in a smooth continuum window, it is optimistic by a factor of roughly three.

I measured S/N three ways across all 379 spectra. In the 3250–3600 Å continuum of the four A-type finalists — a region genuinely free of strong features — STATERR claims S/N ≈ 306–345, while the scatter about a smooth fit gives 82–124. The ratio runs 2.5–4.0.

Measured in 3250–3600 Å, residuals about a cubic fit. The gap is not counting noise: it is flat-field residuals, co-addition and resampling correlations, and scattered-light subtraction — none of which propagate into STATERR.

Practical figure to use. Optical continuum (4000–9000 Å), typical target: S/N ≈ 100–200 per pixel. Near the Balmer break for a bright A star: S/N ≈ 100 per pixel. Below 2500 Å the library degrades steeply and becomes star-dependent — cool stars have almost no flux there, and the 16th-percentile formal S/N in 2000–2500 Å is only 8. If you need a χ² that means anything, inflate STATERR by ~3, or renormalize it against the local scatter in a line-free window.
03

Stellar parameter coverage

Summarize the stellar parameter coverage of the library.

The v2 FITS headers carry no stellar parameters — a regression from v1, which shipped TEFF, LOG_G, LOG_Z, EBMV and DPC keywords plus dereddened and 10-parsec flux columns. Version 2 files contain three columns and no fit keywords at all.

The parameters are not lost, though: section 4 of the delivery readme tabulates them for every star. They come from fitting Castelli (2004) models restricted to Victoria-Regina isochrones (VandenBerg et al. 2006), with surface gravity constrained so the implied distance sits within 2σ of the Hipparcos parallax. I parsed that table back into machine-readable form — 324 of 379 stars have a fit.

The metallicity is [M/H], not [Fe/H]

Worth being precise about, because it changes how the numbers should be used. NGSL reports LOG_Z, documented as “log abundance with respect to solar” — a scaled-solar total metallicity taken from the Castelli model grid, not a measured iron abundance. It comes with a separate boolean α-enhancement flag: 107 of the 324 fitted stars are flagged α-enhanced, the other 217 solar-scaled. The abundance information is therefore two-valued — [M/H] plus a discrete α switch — rather than the [Fe/H] and continuous [α/Fe] a spectroscopic analysis would give.

The scale is nonetheless sound. Against MILES [Fe/H] over the 129 shared stars, NGSL [M/H] sits at a median offset of −0.03 dex with 0.23 dex scatter — no meaningful systematic, though the scatter is wide enough that individual values should not be trusted much better than ~0.25 dex. Where a real [Fe/H] is needed, take it from MILES for the 145 overlap stars.

The hot end is sparse. Only 22 stars exceed 10000 K and none exceed 14204 K, so the library cannot constrain models hotter than mid-B. Coverage is also strongly correlated: nearly all metal-poor stars are warm subgiants and dwarfs, so [M/H] and Teff cannot be varied independently across much of the grid.
04

How the spectra are flux calibrated

Summarize the way the spectra are flux calibrated.

NGSL does not use standard CALSTIS products. The calibration is a custom chain, and its defining problem is that most targets were not centred in the slit. The slit is 0.2″ wide — only 4 pixels — so a mis-centred star loses a wavelength-dependent fraction of its light. Correcting that is what separates v2 from v1 and what sets the library's real accuracy floor.

  1. Custom pipeline extractionNew spectral traces averaged over all NGSL stars; per-visit fringe flats for G750L; background taken 30 px above and below rather than the standard 300 px below; an 11-pixel extraction height instead of 7, trading some S/N for photometric stability; custom dispersion coefficients for the E1 aperture position.
  2. Custom sensitivity curvesBuilt specifically for this background-subtraction method, from observations of the standard star BD+75D325 centred in the 52X0.2E1 aperture. This is the absolute flux anchor of the whole library.
  3. Zero-point wavelength correctionNo wavecals were taken with the stellar exposures, so offsets were derived from the positions of strong stellar features.
  4. Co-addition and mergingTwo exposures per grating at different slit positions, combined with cosmic-ray and hot-pixel rejection, then the three gratings averaged together across the 2990–3060 Å and 5500–5650 Å overlaps.
  5. G230LB scattered-light subtractionIn-order grating scatter, severe for cool stars, modelled as SC = C0(1 + 0.0024x) in the extracted net spectrum. C0 is predicted by integrating the count rate divided by λ³ from 2000 to 10000 Å and scaling by 5523 — calibrated on 103 red library stars with B−V > 1.1 that were themselves well centred.
  6. Slit-throughput correctionThe offset of each star within the slit is measured from the G750L fringe flats, by correlating fringes in the flat against fringes in the stellar spectrum. In v2 the correction is empirical, derived from post-SM4 observations of BD+75D325 stepped across the slit. Below 5700 Å the flux is scaled by a linear-in-wavelength factor normalized to 1.0 at 5300 Å, tuned to drive the spectroscopic B−V onto the Tycho-2 value; above 5700 Å the correction comes from spline fits to NGSL/model flux ratios in 400 Å bins as a function of offset.
Accuracy achieved. The authors validate against the ground-based MILES library: after correction, agreement is ~3% from 3600 to 7300 Å, and the slit correction itself is good to a couple of percent. The correction is only reliable for offsets under 0.9 pixels — beyond that the fringe-flat method saturates, because it starts correlating with the wings of the PSF rather than the core, and the offset can even appear to shrink. Those 35 stars are flagged DATAQUAL = 'suspect', as are stars whose spectroscopic V differs from Tycho-2 by more than 0.1 mag or B−V by more than 0.3. Treat OFFSETPX as a systematic-error proxy, and read it before trusting any spectral shape.

A star for testing the Balmer break

Find a star at around 10000 K that would be good for testing stellar spectral models of the Balmer break shape (~3600 Å).

17 library stars fall between 9000 and 11500 K. Ranking them for this specific purpose means weighting the flux calibration above everything else: the Balmer break is a continuum shape spanning the 3646 Å limit, so a wavelength-dependent throughput error is indistinguishable from the signal being measured. Slit offset is therefore the dominant criterion, ahead of brightness or even S/N.

Applying that, plus rejecting chemically peculiar stars, known binaries and evolved objects: HD205811 is a double-star component (ADS 15147 A); HD072968 and HD170973 are spectrum-peculiar (A1spe, A0sp); HD143459 is a horizontal-branch star with E(B−V) = 0.106 and a 1000 K disagreement between the NGSL and MILES temperatures. All are excluded.

  • Slit offset 0.00 px. The single best-centred star in the temperature range. The wavelength-dependent throughput correction — the dominant systematic on break shape — is essentially null, so the measured continuum slope across 3646 Å is as close to raw instrument calibration as the library gets.
  • Effectively unreddened. B−V = −0.01 against an intrinsic 0.00 for A0, so extinction cannot masquerade as break curvature.
  • Solar metallicity, no α-enhancement. [M/H] = +0.0 with the α flag off and fit quality 1, so a scaled-solar model grid applies directly — no abundance-pattern mismatch folded into the continuum. This is what disqualifies HD128801, at [M/H] = −1.9 and α-enhanced.
  • No multiplicity or peculiarity flags in SIMBAD, unlike three others in the window.
  • S/N ≈ 100 per pixel at the break on the corrected scale, from a well-exposed V = 6.75 target.
One caveat to carry into the fit. The luminosity class is not settled: SIMBAD types HD194453 as A0III (giant), while the NGSL model fit returns log g = 3.9, which is subgiant-to-dwarf. Since the Balmer break is gravity-sensitive — that is much of why it is worth testing — log g should be a free parameter here rather than fixed from the spectral type. If you need a star whose classification and gravity already agree, use HD040573 (B9.5V, log g 4.2, offset 0.36 px, DBalmer within 0.011 mag); if you want one with fully independent published parameters, use HD128801, which is in MILES at 10250 K against NGSL's 10123 K with E(B−V) = 0.026 — though the two catalogs disagree by 0.7 dex on its abundance ([M/H] = −1.9 α-enhanced vs [Fe/H] = −1.20), making it the weakest of the three on composition.

Overlap with MILES and MaStar

Cross-matching all 379 NGSL positions against both libraries within 5″ gives a sharply split answer.

MaStarYan+ 2019 (first release)3622–10354 Å, R ~ 180033260 / 379

Vizier catalogs J/MNRAS/371/703 and J/ApJ/883/175, matched on J2000 positions from the v2 FITS headers.

MILES: 145 stars in common, and they validate each other

This overlap is not incidental — Lindler & Heap used MILES to verify the v2 slit correction in the first place. It also provides an independent parameter check that the delivery documentation does not: over the 129 overlap stars with temperatures in both catalogs, Teff(NGSL) − Teff(MILES) has a median of +59 K with 102 K scatter. Metallicity agrees comparably well: NGSL [M/H] minus MILES [Fe/H] has a median of −0.03 dex with 0.23 dex scatter. Two independently derived scales agreeing that closely is a genuine endorsement of both. MILES also supplies E(B−V) and a true [Fe/H] per star, neither of which v2 provides.

The practical value is that MILES covers 3525–7500 Å at FWHM 2.5 Å — higher resolution than NGSL at the break, and reaching just blueward of it. For any of the 145 shared stars you can cross-check the break against a ground-based, independently calibrated spectrum. None of my top three picks are in MILES, but HD128801 is, which is the strongest argument for it as a secondary target.

MaStar: zero overlap, and the reason is structural

The null result is real, not a matching failure — the closest NGSL star to any MaStar target is 1.4′ away, with a median separation of 7.4°. The cause is selection: MaStar observed stars through SDSS fibres in the MaNGA footprint, targeting r ≈ 12–17, while NGSL stars run V = 1.5–12.2 with a median of 6.5. NGSL targets would saturate the BOSS spectrographs outright. The two libraries were built for the same science from opposite ends of the brightness range and do not meet.

One qualification: the Vizier table is the Yan et al. (2019) first release of 3326 stars. Full MaStar in SDSS DR17 holds roughly 24000 stars, so a DR17 match could in principle turn up a handful. The brightness argument makes it unlikely to be more than that.

What was built

Everything is reproducible from the archive. Data and documentation are gitignored; the scripts regenerate them.

scripts/fetch_ngsl.shDownloads the 5 documentation PDFs and the 18.3 MB spectra archive from MAST
scripts/build_catalog.pyMerges v2 FITS headers, the readme model-fit table and the SIMBAD magnitude table → ngsl_catalog.csv, 379 rows
scripts/measure_snr.pyPer-star S/N in 9 wavelength bands, both estimators → ngsl_snr.csv
scripts/crossmatch_libraries.pyPositional match against MILES and MaStar → ngsl_crossmatch.csv
scripts/coverage.pyParameter-coverage and S/N figures
scripts/balmer_candidates.pyBreak-region plots and DBalmer measurement
scripts/lsf_resolution.pyMeasures true LSF FWHM and resolving power from the STScI LSF files → stis_lsf_resolution.csv
scripts/candidate_table.pyThe five-candidate comparison above → balmer_candidates.csv, joining NGSL fits, measured spectra, MILES parameters and live SIMBAD types
Recovered along the way. The stellar parameters exist only as a text table inside aaareadme.pdf — v2 dropped the header keywords that v1 carried. build_catalog.py parses that table back out, so the library's own Teff, log g and [M/H] are queryable alongside the spectra again.
